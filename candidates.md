# Candidates — The One-Line Cat Joke That Lands

Judge brief (from the kit, published in full): a stand-up comic who has told more
cat jokes than any room should allow. Criteria, most important first:
1. A one-line joke about cats, with a setup and a turn that lands.
2. Laugh out loud on the first read, not merely smile at the cleverness.
3. Surprise, misdirection, or a fresh angle — not a pun heard many times before.
4. Few words: no padding, no wind-up, no explanation after the punchline.
5. A joke a person would actually retell to a friend the same day.

Playbook applied (winners-study): concrete scene + specific detail + strongest
beat last beats abstract wordplay; avoid stock puns ("purrfect", "clawful")
entirely — criterion 3 explicitly penalizes them.

## The drafts

1. I bought my cat a $200 bed; she sleeps in the box it came in, and honestly, at this point I'm the pet — I just pay rent and open cans.
2. My cat stares at the wall for hours like she's watching a thriller; I checked — nothing there. She's just buffering a grudge from 2019.
3. The vet said my cat is perfectly healthy, just "emotionally unavailable" — so now we have something in common besides the hair on my clothes.
4. My cat knocks things off the table while maintaining eye contact, like a tiny mob boss making sure I understand the message.
5. Dogs have owners, cats have staff — mine just filed a complaint with HR because breakfast was four minutes late.
6. My cat ignores me all day, then sits on my laptop the second I open it — she's not affectionate, she's just management doing a surprise audit.
7. **I taught my cat to high-five, and now she uses it exclusively to slap treats out of my hand before I can change my mind.**
8. My cat screams at 3 a.m. for food she already has, in a bowl she can see — she's not hungry, she's just reviewing my service.
9. Every morning my cat sits on my chest and stares until I wake up; it's less "good morning" and more a debt collector who's found my address.
10. I spent $40 on a cat toy and my cat played with the receipt; economists call this a recession indicator, I call it Tuesday.
11. My cat has a $300 cat tree, three beds, and my pillow — she sleeps on my clean laundry, because the only amenity she values is my suffering.
12. My cat brought me a dead mouse, looked me dead in the eye, and I swear she was checking whether I'd finally learned to hunt.

## Duel scoring (each pair judged in both orders against the criteria)

- #5 is eliminated early: "cats have staff" is a famous old line — criterion 3
  kills it no matter how fresh the HR extension is.
- #10 and #11 lose on criterion 4: two-clause wind-ups, and the laugh is a
  smile at the framing, not a first-read LOL (criterion 2).
- #1 vs #11: #1 wins — the "I'm the pet" turn is one clean beat; #11 sprawls.
  But #1's box-bed premise is a very familiar internet observation, so it
  stalls on criterion 3 against the finalists.
- #3 vs #6: both are "smile at cleverness" jokes. #6 (management audit) is the
  fresher angle; #3's turn ("something in common") is softer. #6 advances.
- #2 vs #8: #8 wins — "reviewing my service" is a sharper single beat than
  "buffering a grudge," which takes a second to process.
- #4 vs #9: #9 wins — the debt-collector image escalates; "tiny mob boss" is
  a framing seen often in memes (criterion 3).
- Semifinal #6 vs #7: #7 wins big. #6 is clever; #7 is physical comedy — a
  trained trick weaponized against the trainer. The surprise is an action,
  not a label, so it laughs on first read (criterion 2) instead of after a
  beat of processing.
- Semifinal #8 vs #9: #9 wins narrowly — chest-sitting at dawn is the more
  universal, retellable scene.
- Final #7 vs #9 (both orders): #7 wins both ways. #9's punch is an analogy
  ("like a debt collector") — the reader compares two things. #7's punch is
  an event: the slap actually happens, mid-scene, and the last clause ("before
  I can change my mind") reveals the cat anticipated the human's regret, which
  is the second laugh stacked on the first. Cleanest setup-and-turn of the
  field, no pun, no padding, nothing after the punchline, and instantly
  retellable.

## Winner

> I taught my cat to high-five, and now she uses it exclusively to slap treats out of my hand before I can change my mind.

Setup (a wholesome training success) → turn (the skill is used only for
robbery) → kicker (the cat knows you'll chicken out). 120 characters, inside
the kit's 1–200 character gate for /output/entry.txt.
